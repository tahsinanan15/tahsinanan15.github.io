# Design: Tahsin OS

A 1995 skeleton in a 2007 skin. Putty bevels, file names and status bars from the beige Windows era, with Aero glass, gel and sky on top. Y2K toys stay small: the media player and the games.

## The idea
The desktop looks back. Observation is her one skill, so the Start button is an eye that follows the pointer, boot opens like an eye and shutdown closes it, and OBSERVE.EXE analyses the visit, tests her two headlines and segments her paintings.

## Colour
Aero sky and grass carry the surface. Sheets stay white so the work stays calm.
- Sky: `--sky-900 #06306b`, `--sky-700 #0a5cb8`, `--sky-500 #1f8fe8`, `--sky-300 #7fd0ff`, `--sky-100 #e4f4ff`
- Grass: `--grass-700 #156f20`, `--grass-500 #2f9e36`, `--grass-300 #9be05a`
- Putty: `--putty-1 #f6f1e6` to `--putty-4 #8f8672`
- Ink: `--ink #0f2238`, `--ink-2 #3f5268`. Accent and focus ring: `--pink #c7118c`
- Chart series, in fixed order, CVD-validated: `#0b6fd0 #c96a00 #6a3fc9 #2c8f24 #d6189a`
- Scenes (`data-scene`): day, sunset and night retint the glass and taskbar only. Sheets stay white.

## Type
- Baloo Da 2 for headings: soft and rounded, and a Bengali type family, so the greeting and the headings share a voice.
- Hind Siliguri for body: clean and humanist, in the Frutiger spirit.
- Silkscreen for file names and LCD readouts, at 16px or 8px so the pixels stay crisp.
- Monospace appears only for the real event log.
- Each other OS has one display face for big headings and the Start button: Retro Stereo Wide, CAT Liebing Gotisch, Data Trash, Indira K, Alte Haas Grotesk. Small text stays in that OS's reading face. Sources and licenses are in `assets/fonts/LICENSES.txt`.

## Materials
- Glass: title bars and taskbar. Gradient with a hard gloss line at 50%, backdrop blur, one slow light sweep on the focused window.
- Putty: window frames, toolbars, tabs, status bars, scrollbar tracks.
- Gel: primary buttons in green, secondary in white-blue. Green text is white and stays above 4.5:1.
- Chrome: media player and game bezels only. LCD: dark panel, cyan pixel text. Campaign results use it too.

## Motion
One authored moment: the eyelids. Windows pop open with an exponential ease-out over 420ms. Bubbles rise slowly at about 30fps. Reduced motion removes the travel and keeps everything readable.

### The frame clock
Everything that moves runs on one frame loop, `js/clock.js`, and nothing else asks the browser for frames. The visualizer and the song's live levels, the bubbles, the wallpaper's ripple and breathing, the play-along game (still through `deck.game`), Panopticon, Bubble Pop, the eyelids, the Start eye and the player settling after the game opens all join it. Each frame goes levels first, then every measurement, then every change, so the page is never laid out twice in a frame. Sizes and places are measured once and kept until a resize or a new look, and whether something is on screen comes from the browser (`seen`), not from reading layout.
- Two speeds. The calm things (bubbles, bars, wallpaper) run at 30 frames a second on every screen. The game, the two toys while they're played, and windows on the move get every frame the display has.
- It sleeps, asking for no frames at all: while the tab is hidden, while nothing that moves is on screen (a phone's sheet over the desktop), and after 20 seconds with no pointer, tap, scroll or key and no music. Before sleeping the calm motion slows to rest over about a second, and `<html>` gets `.is-asleep`, which pauses the CSS loops (a blink or a title bar sweep under way finishes moving first). The first visit's call to the tape, bubbles streaming into it included, keeps going through its minute. Any input, or music starting, wakes it at once. It never sleeps while music plays, the tape runs, a level is on, or the kitty is showing them around. The kitty starts no stroll or pastime it couldn't finish before the clock dozes, and if the clock falls asleep while it is on its way somewhere anyway, it sits down where it is.
- A screen that can't hold the calm frames near 30 a second for a few seconds gets less to draw, a step at a time and never back during the visit: first the wallpaper stops rippling, then there are half as many bubbles on a coarser canvas.
- The ripple is drawn at the screen's own size on desktops, as before (at half size the detailed wallpapers went soft). Phones never ripple: their wallpaper only breathes.
- Reduced motion is unchanged: still bubbles, no ripple, no travel. Something new that moves joins with `join({ rate, read, write })`; the module's header says how.

## Layout
The base styles are a plain scrolling page, used only without JavaScript. With JavaScript the page is always `.os`: windows over a wallpaper. Only her photo and the media player start open, so the desktop is clear and PLAY_ME.EXE is the first thing a new visitor notices. `placeStart()` computes where every window will land.

Under 980 wide or 540 tall the head script in `index.html` adds `.phone` before first paint, and that one class is all that changes when a window crosses the line. A phone is the same OS on the same window manager:
- Home screen: a 4-by-4 grid of the 14 icons, with her photo as a widget in the top two cells of the last column, and the player as a compact widget above the taskbar. Until the tape is played the grid moves down, so the kitty (or, with the kitty off, the handwritten note) has its own band above PLAY_ME.EXE and covers nothing. In landscape the icons take the left and the widgets step aside to the right. On a short portrait screen the player gives up its visualizer first, then its place on the home screen.
- Sheets: an open window fills the screen above the taskbar, with one 44px close button, and its body scrolls inside. `sheets` in `js/main.js` is the stack, and `syncHistory` gives each sheet one history entry, so the back gesture closes the top one and never leaves the site. An open Start menu or dialog takes the back gesture first. Her photo and the player go back to being widgets when closed. The short sheets, Contact and Recycle Bin, sit at the bottom over her painting. Paint's paper is sized when it first opens: as tall as an upright phone has room for, and it keeps that shape if the phone turns.
- The tape: the document on top, the dock below it, and the VCR text riding the dock's title bar. No spotlight. In landscape the dock is the right column. A touch on the document pauses it, and the VCR text and clock say so. On the end card only the home screen is behind the dock, so the dock takes the room it needs, and the tape's icon replays it.

Rules for wide windows are written `.os:where(:not(.phone))`, which keeps the desktop's specificity. Everything phone lives in the last block of `css/site.css`, inside `@media screen` so print is untouched.

## Sound
All effects are synthesized in `js/audio.js`. Music starts on the first gesture, because browsers allow nothing earlier. Mute is always visible in the taskbar.

## Icons
Every OS has its own set of 16 drawings. Aero's are hand-authored gel symbols in `index.html`. The other five live in `assets/icons/<os>.svg` as symbols named `<os>-person`, `<os>-chart` and so on, fetched the first time that OS appears, and `swapIcons` in `js/main.js` points every `<use>` at the current set. If a set can't load, the Aero icons stay up. A new set must change the object, not just the color: the resume is a floppy in Vaporwave OS and a scroll in Baroque Keep. No shared tile or frame behind them.

## Six songs, six operating systems
Same files and same words in every OS. Everything else changes: wallpaper (one of her paintings), palette, type, window chrome, cursor, icons, bubbles, start chime, and what the screen does on the beat. A theme lives in three places: `js/themes.js` (name, wallpaper, visualizer colors), a block in `css/site.css` under `[data-theme=<id>]`, and a world in `js/fx.js`.

| Song | OS | Reference | On the beat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Online Office V4.20 | Frutiger Aero | gel, sky and grass, always Green Hills | wallpaper ripples |
| Glowing Eyes, telepath | Vaporwave OS | pink and cyan, marble, grid | the grid rolls with the bass |
| Musette and Drums, Cocteau Twins | Baroque Keep | gothic castle, Mozart and Beethoven, gold leaf | the room breathes |
| U Weren't Here, Cult Member | Ghost Net | cyberpunk, acid yellow and teal, hard edges | data blocks slip |

| Cities in Dust, Siouxsie and the Banshees | Concrete Jungle | New York, asphalt and taxi yellow | the street lamps breathe |

Wallpapers all come from one recipe in `_dev/build_walls.py`: the original painting, sharpened, never zoomed past its full width, then that OS's grade. Portrait cityscapes show the whole painting repeated along the street. Each OS has its own wallpaper and none is shared.

Her photo follows the song too: the same picture, toned to each OS's palette, in a frame that matches the OS.

The screen moves gently and only with the music. Windows themselves do not move, the checkbox in the media player turns it all off, and reduced motion removes it. Evoke a world, never copy a franchise's logos, characters or art.

## Play along
The star in the media player's screen is a button. It turns the visualizer into a four-lane rhythm game (`js/beats.js`): the screen grows, the bars stay as its floor, and blocks come in on the song's beat. Each song is a level of its first 90 seconds, with up to three stars, kept in the playlist. Keys are A S K L. On a touch screen or with a mouse, tap a lane, or hold down and draw across the lanes to cut every block you cross, so one finger can clear a level. Results go to OBSERVE.EXE's log next to Bubble Pop's. The two arrow glyphs beside the full-screen one, or the left and right arrow keys, move to the level before or after. The eye glyph, or W, is for just watching: the whole song plays and the world goes by with nothing to hit.

The player earns the song. Each song's drums and bass are cut out ahead of time (`_dev/build_backing.py`) and play underneath the whole level. The song itself only sounds while notes keep getting hit: it comes in on the first hit and drops to a whisper on a miss, the way Guitar Hero mutes a guitar. Every hit also sounds the song's own note at that moment, in that OS's voice, the way Piano Tiles plays the tune. Blocks are cut in two, some want a sweep across two lanes, and some come two at a time, one for each hand. Hits within a tenth of a second are perfect. The window to hit at all is a third of a second either way, on purpose: this is for visitors, not players. Bombs come in past the opening, in a lane the other hand isn't using: each world has its own, drawn in VectorCraft (sources in `_dev/bombs`, exported to `assets/beats/bombs` with `vectorcraft-cli convert`): a sea mine, the old system-error bomb in pixels, a gilt iron grenade, a hazard mine, a jute-bound ball in ink, dynamite on a timer. All of them sit over the same blinking red ring, with a spark burning at the fuse. Pressing one costs the streak, the song, 300 points and five notes toward the stars, which is what stops all four keys being mashed. A note to hold is a beaded rail of light with a ring at its far end, and it ticks upward and chimes when it is carried through.

One board, two views. In the player it lies flat. The corner glyph beside the time takes it full screen: the player's screen leaves its window and fills the display, the board tilts back into a road, and the bars line both sides of it to the horizon, low sounds nearest. Escape steps back one view at a time. The same projection draws both. In full screen the road is a ribbon. Once a level is under way, waves travel down it toward the player, the way a shaken-out carpet carries one. Each song sets its waves (`ride` in `js/beatworlds.js`: how tall, how long, how fast): Online Office gets long slow swells, Musette and Drums short fast ones. Nothing moves sideways, the camera holds still, and the waves flatten out before the line, so the pads never move. Only a miss or a bomb shakes the picture.

Each OS is a world (`js/beatworlds.js`), drawn in code: Frutiger Aero is a glass causeway over bright water, Vaporwave OS a grid running to a striped sun, Baroque Keep a red runner down a candlelit nave, Ghost Net a wet street under neon in amber haze, Ray OS a railway through the fields on black and white film, Concrete Jungle an avenue at dusk. A world paints its sky once, keeps a few things moving in it, lays its road, and gives the blocks their shape, sparks and voice. Text stays the LCD's, and the bars keep `THEMES[id].viz`. Under reduced motion the scenery holds still and nothing shakes or flashes. A screen that can't keep up gets a plainer picture on its own.

Charts are built from the audio by `_dev/build_beats.py`, so after a song file changes run `_dev/build_backing.py` and then `_dev/build_beats.py`.

## The kitty
A cat lives on the desktop (`js/kitty.js`). It sits on the taskbar's top edge and behaves like one: it strolls (sometimes with a long stretch first), grooms, yawns, looks around, naps curled up and wakes, and bats at a bubble now and then. A cursor skittering along the floor near it is prey: it crouches, wiggles and pounces. When music plays it nods on the beat. Rest the mouse on it and it shuts its eyes and hearts float up over its head. Click it for three things: show me around, the resume, a nap. Pick it up and it hangs by the scruff and swings with the cursor; let go and it falls and lands. Until someone has played the tape, it is already sitting beside PLAY_ME.EXE when the page opens, pointing at it and saying "psst… start here", while the bubbles gather into the tape; on a phone it sits in the band above the top row, over PLAY_ME.EXE. Its strolls keep to open floor: it never walks across the player or any window that comes down to the taskbar, and with nowhere open to go it stays sat. Its bubble and menu are the OBSERVE balloon's paper in each OS (its colours and heading colour, and the balloon's rounded corners and soft shadow, the same in every OS), with the OS's own buttons. Its nap's z and its ! use the OS's icon-label colour and display face, and its hearts an accent from that OS's palette. Opening it hides the OBSERVE balloon, so there is one bubble at a time, and Escape or a click elsewhere closes its menu. On a landscape phone it lives along the player, never out over the icons.

It plays with the bubbles. Every 40 to 90 seconds at most on a desktop (rarer on a phone, never two bouts back to back), sitting on its floor and free (not in the first-visit hook, the show-around, a carry, the game or the tape), it notices a bubble low over its own stretch of open floor, clear of the icons, the player and any window that comes down to the floor. It looks at it, then pops it with a crouch and a pounce or a leap up under it, or on a desktop bats it along the floor like a ball of yarn (`bubbles.nudge` gives it a push that slows back to the usual drift, kept low and inside that stretch), runs after it, bats it two to four times and pops it or loses interest. The pop is silent and not tracked. The frame clock runs it at full rate only while it walks, leaps or pounces, calm while it watches. On a desktop it is livelier than on a phone: shorter sits, short runs, hops along the floor, now and then a hop up onto the top edge of an open window near it (never the player, never past the icons; it hops down after a while, or at once if the window moves or closes), and naps less often. A bout ends at once if the game or the tape starts. While music plays it bops its head on the beat (frame 0 on the kick, keeping the last tempo it heard between kicks) in stretches of a few bars, about two fifths of the time, and does its other things between. Its walk and run play at 15 and 30 frames a second so the legs step evenly on the clock's frames, and the walk gets every frame on a desktop. Under reduced motion it never chases; a blown bubble in reach gets one swat from sitting.

Windows are perches on a desktop. Dropped over an open window (the player too), it lands on the title bar's top edge instead of falling to the taskbar, and that edge is its floor: it sits, grooms, naps and strolls along it within the window's width, never over the title bar's buttons, and hops down after a minute or two. Its own hops up onto a window use the same perch. If the window moves, closes, is minimised or maximised, or another window comes over its spot, it hops down at once. Phones keep it on the player and taskbar. On every song change (a new OS and coat) it looks around and gives a little hop in place as the new coat lands; asleep, it wakes and looks around. Not in the tape, the game, the first-visit hook, or while carried.

The bubble wand (tray button beside the paw, tooltip "Bubble wand", off by default, remembered as `tahsin.wand`) makes the cursor over the open desktop the OS's own wand, an inline SVG drawn in that OS's icon palette with its ring as the hotspot; windows, icons and the taskbar keep their cursors. A press and drag there blows a short stream of bubbles the way and speed of the drag (a flick blows them furthest), a click blows one or two, and on a phone a sideways finger drag on the home screen does it (a tap is just a tap; with the wand on the icon layer takes `touch-action: pan-y`, so a vertical drag still scrolls the icons). Blown bubbles are drawn like the rest, at most 12 at once (the oldest pops first), slow down and drift; they are the kitty's favourite: one that drifts within about 400 px of a free kitty gets played with within a second. Under reduced motion they still blow and drift gently.

The paw button in the taskbar tray (tooltip "Kitty") and the Start menu's "Kitty: on / off" line both turn it on and off, remembered in this browser (`tahsin.kitty`); it is on by default. Turned off, it walks out by the nearest edge and then does no work at all: it asks the frame clock for no frames and fetches nothing, and on a first visit the handwritten note points at the tape instead. PLAY_ME.EXE keeps its own dock kitty either way, so playing the tape still loads that kitty's sheets. Turned on again, it walks in from the left edge. In the play-along game it says how to play, once. It makes no sound, and a bubble it pops makes none either.

"Show me around" is a guided walk. For each of ABOUT_ME, NCL_CASE, OBSERVE, DESIGN_WORK, MY_ART, RESUME and CONTACT it runs along the floor and hops up the side of the icon grid to sit on the open desktop just right of it, level with the icon, so it covers no icon. It turns to the icon, points at it with one paw (the icon lights up as if selected, so a first-column icon is not mistaken for the one beside it), opens the window and says one plain line about it, its bubble kept clear of the window's title and heading and of the icon. Windows it opens leave a lane between the icons and themselves, so the cat and its bubble sit in open desktop and cover no window text. It moves on after a few seconds, or when the kitty or its Next is clicked, and closes the window it opened. A window the visitor already had open, or minimised, it shows without a sound and leaves open. It ends pointing at PLAY_ME.EXE with a button for the tape. A click anywhere else, Escape, picking it up or starting the tape ends the walk, even halfway to the next icon: it sits down where it is, or hops down from where it climbed. A click inside the window it opened leaves that window open and counts it as the visitor's own open; any other stop closes it. On a phone it steps down onto the taskbar, points, and opens each window as a sheet that stops short of the taskbar, leaving a band where the cat sits and says its line beside itself, so neither covers the sheet; the back gesture ends the walk the way Escape does. For the tape it goes back up onto the player. Its opens and closes are silent and are not counted as the visitor's own; "Her resume" from its menu is the visitor's own open, but silent too.

It is a 3D character rendered to sprites. `_dev/kitty/blender` models a chibi kitten in Blender, keys every action, and renders it flat-shaded with one shadow tone and a dark outline, all from one three-quarter camera; the actions that travel (walk, run, jump, fall, stalk, pounce) turn the cat 30 degrees toward screen-right. It is rendered in six coats, one per OS: a seal-point ragdoll, lilac and cyan, a black cat with gold eyes, a neon-outlined teal cat, black and white film, a ginger alley cat. Each coat has three sheets in `assets/kitty` (move: getting about, and the sit; idle: its pastimes and its nap; play: carried, the hunt, pointing, stroked, the nod; 0.5 to 0.88 MB a coat, under 900 KB each), described by `assets/kitty/kitty.json`, which also holds the walk and run strides. Every frame is a fixed cell, and a point in the cell marks the floor under the cat, so any frame is placed by its feet; a sitting cat that lifts a paw is pinned by the paws still down. The walk and run cycles stay in place in the sheet; the page moves the sprite one stride per cycle, so the paws don't slide. Jumps and falls are moved along their arc by the page, with the sheet's crouch, rise, fall and landing frames.

PLAY_ME.EXE has a small kitty of its own in the narration dock: it purrs on the first episode, points at each window, and nods at the end. On a phone it has its own small column beside the dock's text, so it covers none of the document or its buttons. The desktop kitty hides during the tape, under a phone's sheets (except while it is showing them around) and behind the full-screen game. Under reduced motion it sits still, moves straight to where it is going, and can still be carried and asked for help. Its current behaviour is on the element as `data-state`. Nothing of it loads until the page is up and idle: then kitty.json and its move sheet, its idle sheet when the page is idle again, and its play sheet only when it is first wanted (the mouse on it, a pick-up, prey, a nod, the show-around or the first-visit hook); until then it does without those moves. Another OS's coat loads when that OS is shown, and until each of its sheets is in, the kitty goes on in its old coat. It draws only when its picture changes (8 times a second while it sits), reads no layout while it moves, and stops altogether when nothing changes; changes between clips that don't follow on crossfade over three frames. A first visit loads about 0.80 MB of it in the Aero coat (the script, kitty.json and all three sheets, since it points at the tape at once); a returning visit loads about 0.57 MB until its play sheet is first wanted. Each sheet is decoded once, off the main thread, and kept decoded, so drawing never decodes it again. On a phone with its CPU slowed 4x (22 paired runs on a rented test machine) it adds about 1 point of main-thread script time and about 4.5 points of total busy time, against run-to-run noise of a few points; a walk glides as a browser animation, off the main thread, and it carries no CSS filter. The dock kitty draws at its clip's rate and stops on a held pose.

## PLAY_ME.EXE
A 30-second tour for recruiters. It answers three things: who is she, what can she do, why hire her. Until someone has played it, the tape bounces and glows, the bubbles spiral into it, and the kitty sits beside it and points at it. Clicking pops the bubbles and starts the tape. If they open something else or change the song, it calms down until the next visit. It plays as an old tape: cassette-deck icon, blue screen and tracking bars on power-up, tape hiss and motor from `vhs` in `js/audio.js`, and a shutter skip between steps. It restores the desktop exactly as it found it.
